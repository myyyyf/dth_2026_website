# OpenRefine for Historical Research

[OpenRefine](https://openrefine.org/) is an open source tool for cleaning and organizing data. Originally created by David Huynh at Metaweb Technologies, it is now maintained by a community of contributors.

## Applications in Historical Research

Historians often work with records from various resources. A researcher studying Bronze Age artefacts might collect 2,000 records from different museums. Similar objects can be labelled "bronze vessel", "ritual vessel" or "bronze container". In such circumstances,  OpenRefine's clustering function can effectively identify similar terms, allowing researchers to standardize object names after reviewing them. The software is also helpful to detect possible duplicates and inconsistent information. The cleaned data could then be exported for comparison across periods or regions.

## My Evaluation

I would use OpenRefine for a history thesis, especially when cleaning research data. Its active development and open licence make it a efficient choice, although uncertain funding raises concerns about long-term maintenance. Since datasets can be exported, researchers could also switch their work to another tool if necessary.
